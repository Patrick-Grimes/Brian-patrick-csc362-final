    Using this visualization

    Keyboard

    • Use Tab to move focus; Enter or Space activates the Help button when focus is on the page background, zooms into a focused county, opens the comparison panel for a focused marker, and shows or refreshes the details tooltip on the community-type bars (tooltips also appear when you tab onto a bar). The +/− controls are standard buttons. Escape closes this dialog when open, and dismisses the first-visit banner at the bottom when the dialog is closed. The map is primarily pointer-based; full geographic pan is not keyboard-complete.
    • Place markers are only in the Tab order after zoom is high enough for them to appear on the map (about 3×).
    • After you load a place in the comparison panel, focus moves to that comparison card (or Tab to it). When the card is focused, use Arrow keys, Page Up/Page Down, Home, and End to scroll if the content is taller than the panel.

    How to navigate

    • Zoom the map using the + and − controls (lower left), the scroll wheel or trackpad, or pinch-to-zoom on touch devices.
    • Click anywhere on a county to zoom deeper into it. With the keyboard, Tab to a county, then Enter or Space.
    • When zoom reaches about 3× or higher, place markers appear. Each marker shows how many banner Dollar General stores are grouped at that Census place (1, 2, etc.). This project does not include other chains (e.g. Dollar Tree) or formats absent from the dataset.
    • Hover a marker to outline that community on top of its county shading. Click a marker to load the comparison charts and narrative in the panel on the right. With the keyboard, Tab to a marker (same highlight as hover), then Enter or Space.
    • To compare averages for rural versus urban communities, scroll to average store density by community type further down this page. Tab to each bar for a tooltip; Enter or Space also shows it.

    What the metrics mean

    • Place density: Dollar General stores per 10,000 residents for that Census place (people-based, not land area).
    • Adjusted county density: the county-wide stores-per-10k figure after excluding this place's stores — so you're comparing how saturated the broader county is versus that specific community.
    • Density gap vs. adjusted county: place density minus adjusted county density, in stores per 10,000 residents. A + means more stores per 10k than the adjusted county; a − means fewer.
    • Statewide gap percentile: where this place's gap ranks among all North Carolina places in the dataset. A higher percentile means a larger gap (more stores in the place relative to its adjusted county) compared with other places.
    • Urban vs. rural: a place is Urban if at least 51% of its 2020 Census population lives in a Census-defined urban area; otherwise it is Rural.
