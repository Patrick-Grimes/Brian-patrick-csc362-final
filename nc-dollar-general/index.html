NC Dollar General Saturation Analysis

Banner-brand Dollar General stores in North Carolina — counts exclude other retail chains and formats not in this dataset (for example Dollar Tree).

Research question: In Census communities with higher Dollar General density than their adjusted county average, is the place poverty rate higher than that county’s poverty rate? We visualize store density and poverty side by side for comparison.

    Using this visualization

    Keyboard

    • Use Tab to move focus; Enter activates the Help button, map markers, and zoom. Escape closes this dialog when open. The map is primarily pointer-based; full geographic pan is not keyboard-complete.

    How to navigate

    • Zoom the map using the + and − controls (lower left), the scroll wheel or trackpad, or pinch-to-zoom on touch devices.
    • Click anywhere on a county to zoom deeper into it.
    • When zoom reaches about 3× or higher, place markers appear. Each marker shows how many banner Dollar General stores are grouped at that Census place (1, 2, etc.). This project does not include other chains (e.g. Dollar Tree) or formats absent from the dataset.
    • Hover a marker to outline that community on top of its county shading. Click a marker to load the comparison charts and narrative in the panel on the right.
    • To compare averages for rural versus urban communities, scroll to average store density by community type further down this page.

    Visual walkthrough

    What the metrics mean

    • Place density: Dollar General stores per 10,000 residents for that Census place (people-based, not land area).
    • Adjusted county density: the county-wide stores-per-10k figure after excluding this place's stores — so you're comparing how saturated the broader county is versus that specific community.
    • Density gap vs. adjusted county: place density minus adjusted county density, in stores per 10,000 residents. A + means more stores per 10k than the adjusted county; a − means fewer.
    • Statewide gap percentile: where this place's gap ranks among all North Carolina places in the dataset. A higher percentile means a larger gap (more stores in the place relative to its adjusted county) compared with other places.
    • Urban vs. rural: a place is Urban if at least 51% of its 2020 Census population lives in a Census-defined urban area; otherwise it is Rural.
